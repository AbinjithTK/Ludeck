Ludeck — the branch tree, three ways

Every colour, size and type step comes from tokens.dart (palette plus the shipped biolume skin). The drawing style follows the Liven reference: smooth tapered branches, small teardrop leaves, two tones. It has been adapted to Ludeck's dark sky and to its rule that leaves never brown or fall. Leaves are decoration. Games are the small cover chips at branch tips. Same data in every option: 5 branches, 2 of them nested.

A · Vine: a vertical tree that expands in place

The trunk runs down the left edge and each named branch curls out to the right. Tapping a branch grows its sub-branches and leaves right there on the screen, like a file tree drawn as a plant. It scrolls like a list.

Home, "Couch co-op" open. Its two sub-branches and their games are grown in place. The tray says 3 games need a branch, so new finds don't disappear.
Create and name a branch. Tap "+ branch" and a twig sprouts with the name field already focused. The starter chips come from the Stage 1 research (mood, session length, who with).
Move a game. Long-press a cover and drag it onto any branch, which lights up as a target. Drop it and choose Move or Also add here. The toast has Undo. Haptic tick on pick-up and on drop.

B · Canopy: a radial tree you zoom into

It looks like the Liven picture. The trunk rises from the bottom and named branches fan out upward. Tapping a branch zooms into it, so that branch becomes the trunk and its sub-branches fan out the same way. The home screen is also the share image.

Home. The whole library at a glance, labels on every branch, cover chips at the tips. Up to 6 branches show and extras fold into a "+2" twig. "What should I play?" sits at the root.
Zoomed into "Couch co-op". Its sub-branches fan out and the covers are bigger. Breadcrumb back to the root. Pick, Share and Add sit on this branch because this is where you're looking.
Pick from this branch. This runs the unused choosePick. The branch rustles once (a 600 ms response to the tap, never idle motion) and one cover rises. You get "Play this" or "Another", and the reason is shown.

C · Trunk and drawers: a list with a painted spine

The most conventional of the three. A drawn trunk runs down the left side, and each branch is a drawer card hanging off it. Opening a drawer shows sub-branch pills and a grid of covers. It is the easiest to read and the least likely to be shared.

Home. Drawers show the name, a count and stacked covers. It reads instantly and scales to 30 branches.
Drawer open. Sub-branch pills act as filters, games show as a grid, and you add a game or sub-branch inline. You get more done here but it looks less like a tree.

Shared by all three · sharing a branch

What gets shared is a branch, not one game. The card only carries what the privacy rule allows: title, cover, status, rating and branch name. It never includes notes, "recommended by" or the channel.

Story card, 9:16. The branch drawn in the same art style, with its covers and one line of text. The link opens the branch.
A friend opens it. They see the branch and one button: Grow this on my tree. The copy keeps credit ("from @abin"), and games they already own merge in instead of duplicating.

Recommendation: B, Canopy, with A's in-place naming and drag

  • B wins on the thing that makes Ludeck different. The home screen and the share image are the same drawing. A and C are good file browsers, and plenty of apps already have one.
  • B's real risk is reading at a glance. Radial layouts fall apart past about 6 branches. The fix is built into the design: at most 6 visible, extras fold into a "+N" twig, and every level is its own zoomed canopy, so no screen ever has more than 6 labels. Plus a "list" toggle in the header that falls back to C for power users.
  • Borrowed from A: sprouting-twig naming and drag-to-branch with Move or Also add, which work the same on a canopy.
  • Difference from the Liven image: no falling or amber leaves. DECISIONS.md says foliage never browns or sheds, and nothing moves at rest. The teal leaves are the biolume skin's.
  • Cost: B is the hardest to build (tapered-Bézier painter, radial layout, zoom transition). About 1.5× A. It's worth it only if the render passes a check by eye on the device, which is the Stage 4 gate.
